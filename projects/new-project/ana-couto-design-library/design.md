## Design register

Register: brand.

This ecosystem treats design as the product and the argument. The pages are not neutral containers for information; they perform the strategic thesis: branding creates value when strategy, experience and communication become one coherent system.

## Visual principles

1. Start with a point of view, not decoration.
   The pages use large declarative statements before details. The visual system feels editorial and strategic before it feels commercial.

2. Use contrast as positioning.
   Ana Couto pages often rely on restrained high-contrast composition. Valometry uses a drenched neon green field. LAJE leans toward strong educational/product blocks. The common pattern is not one palette, but decisive contrast.

3. Let cases become proof systems.
   Case pages are long visual narratives: challenge, strategy, creation, experience, communication, then execution proof. The design supports evidence, not only mood.

4. Preserve brand-specific worlds.
   Each case page allows the client brand to dominate visually. Consul becomes colorful, Brazilian and domestic. Ultragaz preserves blue and energy cues. Herbissimo uses its own product-world assets. The Ana Couto frame stays present but does not overpower the case.

5. Use design to translate method.
   Repeated pattern: strategy becomes visual/verbal universe, then applications, then proof. This is useful for brand decks and brandbooks.

## Color strategy

Ana Couto institutional:

- Restrained black/white/neutral base with strong typographic contrast.
- Occasional bold campaign/case color from client work.
- Color is not decorative; it is often proof of brand system.

Valometry:

- Drenched neon lime as signature surface.
- Black for authority and CTA.
- White/off-white for explanatory cards.
- Beige/taupe for human/consultative diagrams.
- Best use: data, brand analytics, dashboards, diagnostic offers.

LAJE:

- Education/product feel with strong blocks and practical hierarchy.
- Better suited to course pages, frameworks, modules and expert-led content.

Case pages:

- Let client color systems lead.
- Avoid forcing Ana Couto colors into client case presentations.
- Use page frame, type scale and sequencing as the consistent system.

## Typography

Observed direction:

- Large, heavy headlines.
- Short line breaks.
- Minimal decorative typography.
- Body copy supports strategic explanation and case narrative.
- Valometry pairs assertive sans headlines with a more editorial support voice.

Practical rule:

- Use one strong display sans for headlines.
- Use measured body copy with generous line height.
- Keep body text around 65-75 characters.
- Build hierarchy through scale and weight, not through many colors.

## Layout patterns

Ana Couto institutional:

- Big statements.
- Sparse navigation.
- Large vertical rhythm.
- Case cards and proof points.
- Footer with official channels.

Case pages:

- Hero with project name and tagline.
- Category tags such as strategy, experience, brand, positioning, visual/verbal universe.
- Sections named around project logic: challenge, strategy, creation, experience, communication.
- Large image blocks and full-width proof visuals.
- Technical credits at the end.

Valometry:

- Neon field as memorability layer.
- Hero card with isometric analytics illustration.
- Arrow-led scroll rhythm.
- Black sections with green arrow bullets.
- Pill CTAs.
- Tabs for feature explanation.
- Diagrammatic "how it works" section.

LAJE:

- Course/product narrative.
- Module structure.
- Expert bios.
- FAQ and conversion blocks.
- Useful for turning method into curriculum or commercial offer.

## Components to reuse

- Declarative hero: one strong claim, one support paragraph, one visual proof.
- Strategic section titles: "Desafio", "Estrategia", "Criacao", "Experiencia", "Comunicacao".
- Case proof strip: visual assets, applications and short explanation.
- Method tabs: use for BVS, ondas de valor, funil, atributos or equivalent framework parts.
- Arrow bullets: better than generic bullets when the brand wants movement.
- Pill CTA: direct, high-contrast, no decorative gradient.
- Brand moments board: map journey points where the identity must be experienced.

## Practical application recipes

### Branding proposal

Use Ana Couto's structure:

1. Business challenge.
2. Diagnosis.
3. Brand strategy.
4. Experience and journey.
5. Visual/verbal universe.
6. Communication.
7. Implementation and governance.

Design style:

- Start editorial and strategic.
- Bring visual intensity only after the diagnosis.
- Let each deliverable prove business value.

### Brandbook

Recommended order:

1. Context and challenge.
2. Strategic platform.
3. Audiences and journey.
4. Brand moments.
5. Verbal universe.
6. Visual universe.
7. Applications.
8. Governance.
9. Metrics.

Design style:

- Avoid a generic logo manual.
- Treat the brandbook as a decision system.
- Show right/wrong examples only after showing the principle.

### Dashboard or analytics landing page

Use Valometry as reference:

1. Drenched signature color.
2. Big value claim.
3. Simple explanation of the data model.
4. Feature tabs.
5. Proof and audience use cases.
6. Direct contact CTA.

Design style:

- Use diagrams instead of decorative charts.
- Make data feel actionable, not merely impressive.
- Keep CTA black or dark against high-energy color.

### Course or method page

Use LAJE as reference:

1. Outcome headline.
2. Module structure.
3. Expert authority.
4. Practical deliverables.
5. Community/live support.
6. FAQ and conversion.

Design style:

- Make learning feel structured and applied.
- Avoid academic heaviness.
- Show modules as transformation steps.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Do not reduce the method to a logo presentation.
- Do not use generic SaaS blue/purple gradients for brand analytics.
- Do not make all cases visually identical.
- Do not force identical card grids for every service.
- Do not use decorative glassmorphism.
- Do not use gradient text.
- Do not let visual identity appear before strategic diagnosis in a client deck.
- Do not overuse neon green outside Valometry-inspired contexts.

## Final working principle

The strongest reusable lesson from this ecosystem: design should prove the strategy. In Ana Couto-style work, the visual system is not the starting point; it is the visible consequence of diagnosis, positioning, experience and communication.
